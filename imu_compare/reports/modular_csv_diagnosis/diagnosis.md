### 2.1 Analysis of the 31.225 s Event (Two-Wheel Same-Side Liftoff)
At $t = 31.225\text{ s}$:
- $u = 17.90\text{ m/s}$, $a_y = -25.98\text{ m/s²}$ ($-2.65\text{ g}$), $r = -2.59\text{ rad/s}$.
- **Normal Loads**: `fz_fl` = 2367.5 N, `fz_fr` = **0.0 N**, `fz_rl` = 2829.4 N, `fz_rr` = **0.0 N**.
- **Root Cause**:
  1. Both right-side wheels lift off completely ($F_z = 0$). With track width $t_w = 1.174\text{ m}$ and CG height $h_{cg} = 0.33\text{ m}$, rollover threshold is $a_{y,\text{roll}} = \frac{t_w}{2 h_{cg}} g = 17.45\text{ m/s²}$ ($1.78\text{ g}$).
  2. Because the indoor TTC Pacejka model had peak friction $\mu_y \approx 2.5$ and aerodynamic downforce was double-counted (see Section 2.3), the car reached $26\text{ m/s²}$ ($2.65\text{ g}$), causing complete lateral load transfer.
  3. Furthermore, in `ChassisModel.gd` line 81: `m_veh_planar.x += f_veh.y * h_rc_m` **added** the roll-center height to the CG arm rather than reducing it, exaggerating the overturning roll moment by ~15%.

### 2.2 Analysis of the 33.750 s Event (41.75 m/s² Lateral Acceleration Spike)
At $t = 33.750\text{ s}$:
- $u = 3.04\text{ m/s}$, $v = -3.68\text{ m/s}$ (chassis sliding sideways at sideslip angle $\beta = -50.5°$).
- The vehicle slammed back down from the two-wheel roll excursion onto the left wheels, causing normal load on the rear-left wheel to spike to **$F_z = 4628.4\text{ N}$**.
- In `PacejkaMF62TireModel.gd`, dimensionless load offset $df_z = (F_z - F_{z0})/F_{z0} = (4628 - 700)/700 = +5.61$. Because $df_z$ was not clamped to the TTC calibration envelope ($F_z \le 1600\text{ N} \implies df_z \le 1.28$), polynomial/hyperbolic shift terms exploded:
  $$S_{Vy} = F_z ((p_{Vy1} + p_{Vy2} df_z) + \dots) \implies |F_y| = 6591.5\text{ N}$$
- This runaway $6591.5\text{ N}$ force on a single tire produced the nonphysical $a_y = 41.75\text{ m/s²}$ ($4.26\text{ g}$) acceleration spike.

### Analytical Instability Proof (Explicit Euler Wheel Spin ODE)
In `WheelRotationalDynamics.step_wheel_rotation`:
$$I_w \frac{d\omega}{dt} = T_{\text{drive}} - T_{\text{brake}} - F_x R$$
Under forward Euler discretization:
$$\omega_{k+1} = \omega_k - \frac{\Delta t R}{I_w} F_x(\omega_k)$$
Linearizing $F_x$ at $u = 0$: $F_x \approx K_x \kappa \approx K_x \frac{\omega R}{\varepsilon}$, where $K_x \approx 18 F_z$:
$$\omega_{k+1} = \left(1 - \frac{\Delta t \cdot K_x \cdot R^2}{I_w \cdot \varepsilon}\right) \omega_k = (1 - \lambda \Delta t) \omega_k$$
For numerical stability, $|1 - \lambda \Delta t| < 1 \implies \lambda \Delta t < 2.0$. Evaluating with log parameters ($\Delta t = 0.0025\text{ s}$, $I_w = 0.32\text{ kg m²}$, $R = 0.20\text{ m}$, $\varepsilon = 0.25\text{ m/s}$):
- **Front Wheels ($F_z = 661.6\text{ N}$)**: $\lambda \Delta t = 14.89 \gg 2.0$ (**Unstable by 7.4x**).
- **Rear Wheels ($F_z = 808.4\text{ N}$)**: $\lambda \Delta t = 18.19 \gg 2.0$ (**Unstable by 9.1x**).

Because $\lambda \Delta t > 2$, explicit Euler oscillates with exponentially growing amplitude until it hits the Magic Formula saturation boundary ($F_{x,\text{max}} \approx 1.45 F_z = 959\text{ N}$ front, $1172\text{ N}$ rear), producing a permanent 200 Hz period-2 limit cycle.
